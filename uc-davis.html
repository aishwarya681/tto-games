How the TTO functions

The TTO sits within the Office of Research, reporting toward the Vice Chancellor for Research. The Oct 2, 2025 reorg renamed the former InnovationAccess unit to TTO, spun industry contracting (SRAs, MTAs, CDAs) into a new Research and Industry Sponsored Agreements (RAISA) unit led by Eugene Sisman (the TTO's former interim director), and created a separate Operations unit for finance/HR/IT. The TTO itself runs a Utility (general patent) team and a distinct Plant Variety Licensing team (Assistant Director Isaac Rainwater) reflecting UC Davis's agriculture/plant-sciences strength.

Likely pain points & why

Brand-new director navigating a freshly split office
Tudor was appointed Director as part of the Oct 2, 2025 reorg -- about 10 months into the role as of today, in an office that was simultaneously renamed and had its industry-contracting function carved out into a new unit. Real onboarding/process friction is likely as workflow ownership between TTO and RAISA is still settling.
Declining licensing conversion despite steady disclosure volume
FY2023 to FY2024: disclosures rose slightly (124 to 152), but utility licenses issued dropped by half (12 to 6), patents issued fell (41 to 34), and applications filed dropped sharply (199 to 157) -- a plausible sign of triage/throughput strain during the leadership transition, worth asking about directly rather than assuming.
Federal grant slowdown flagged by UC Davis's own Office of Research
UC Davis's own guidance notes NIH awards “declined more than 50% year-over-year in early FY2026,” driven by suspended peer-review panels and processing backlogs. UC Davis is explicitly telling faculty to diversify toward industry/foundation funding, which puts more translational/licensing pressure directly on the TTO.
