How the TTO functions

OITT is one of four co-equal divisions within ORIED (Offices of Research, Innovation, & Economic Development), alongside Research and Proposal Development, Research Integrity and Compliance, and the Student Research Center -- meaning tech transfer is a named, first-class function here, unlike most other CSU campuses. Its mission: “manage, protect, and commercialize Sacramento State Intellectual Property.” However, its own “Meet Us” page currently shows the Technology Transfer Officer position vacant, staffed instead by an outside IP/Patent Advisor (an emeritus professor/attorney) plus student interns from UC Davis law/PhD programs.

Likely pain points & why

Possible vacancy in the Technology Transfer Officer role itself
If the current staff page is accurate, this is about as concrete a “we need help processing our pipeline” signal as exists among the CSU campuses researched.
Apparent AVP-level leadership turnover
An Interim AVP was quoted in Feb 2025 coverage and an active job posting exists for the permanent AVP role -- a TTO reporting into a leadership chain that's itself in flux typically means slower decision-making and less appetite for new vendor evaluations until the seat is filled.
Severe, very recent budget crisis
A $37M deficit (revised up from an initial $33M estimate) forced elimination of 28 administrative positions (15 terminations) and roughly 22% cuts across most divisions as of April 2025 -- a plausible direct explanation for why OITT's team page shows a vacant TTO role and unfilled leadership.
Freshly earned, first-ever R2 status (Feb 2025)
Sac State hit R2 criteria for the first time with $28M in research funding and 26 doctorates awarded -- research/IP output is rising exactly when the office meant to commercialize it may be understaffed.

In the news / recent involvement

Feb 2025 -- Sac State's first-ever R2 Carnegie Classification, with quotes from President Luke Wood and the Interim AVP.
Spring 2025 -- $37M budget deficit disclosed, leading to 28 eliminated administrative positions.
OITT actively promotes two specific licensable technologies: an Exosome Mimicking Nanovesicles invention (co-developed with UC Davis) and a Photo-Patterned Liquid Crystal Technology (co-developed with Kent State).
